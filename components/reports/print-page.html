Section 2

Where the margin went

Gross margin fell from 63.0% to 61.2% while revenue grew by 12%. Two causes explain almost all of the movement, and both are within our control.

Onboarding effort

Custom setup work now takes an average of 31 hours per new account, up from 22 hours a year ago. Enterprise customers drive most of the increase because they request integrations that fall outside the standard package.

We priced these requests as one-off projects, but only about half were invoiced. The remainder was absorbed into the subscription, which is why revenue looks healthy while delivery cost climbs.

Pricing leakage

Seven of the ten largest renewals closed at last year's list price. Input costs rose 6% over the same period, so every one of those contracts now earns a thinner margin than the one it replaced.

Support coverage

The ticket backlog fell by 18% over the quarter. That improvement relied on temporary contractors, whose cost sits in cost of sales until their contracts end in January.

Without them, we estimate the backlog would have grown by roughly 10%. Replacing the cover with permanent staff costs less per ticket but needs to be approved this year.

Method and data

Margin is measured on invoiced revenue against delivery and support cost recorded in the finance ledger. Contractor invoices were allocated by ticket volume; onboarding hours come from the time-tracking export.

What we expect next

If scope is held and indexation is applied at renewal, margin returns to about 64% by the end of the second quarter. The sensitivity table in the appendix shows the range.
